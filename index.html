Introduction

1M views. 0 clients.
Then $7,000.

I had the attention. I did not have a single client. This guide is about what changed.

For months, I made funny “designer vs developer” videos on Instagram. Some of them reached almost 3 million views. They brought me zero clients.

People came for the joke, not for a website. The views made me feel successful, but no business owner ever wrote to me.

So I stopped posting and went looking for clients directly. This is what failed first:

  • Cold emails with no portfolio. Nobody answered.
  • A beginner portfolio template I bought. It looked cheap, and nobody cared.
  • Writing to every kind of business: hairdressers, restaurants, anyone. My message spoke to no one.

Then I understood the real problem: I had no niche.

I picked one industry, construction. I built a simple portfolio for it, wrote a case study for construction companies, and emailed only them.

Out of 50 companies, one answered. That answer became a call, then a $7,000 project delivered in two months. Today, that website keeps bringing my client new, qualified leads every month.

The client did not pay for a design. They paid for more clients.

What this guide is, and what it is not

This guide is the path I followed, step by step, with the templates I use. It is based on one real deal, not on a magic formula. Your results will depend on your niche, your work, and how many companies you contact.

What you get is a clear path: where to find clients, what to send them, what to say on a call, how to price, and how to get paid.

How to use this guide

  1. Read it once, from start to finish. It takes about an hour.
  2. Open the portfolio template that comes with this guide, and customize it during Steps 1 and 2.
  3. Follow the 30-day action plan at the end. One small task a day.
  4. Use the built-in tools. Every template here fills itself in: type your details once, and the [brackets] update everywhere.

The 8 steps

Remember this

01 Step one

Pick a niche

Clients do not hire “a web designer”. They hire someone who understands their business.

A niche is one type of client you speak to. When I wrote to every kind of business, my message was vague. When I wrote only to construction companies, my portfolio, my case study and my email all said the same thing to the same person.

Why construction worked for me

  • One client is worth a lot. My client’s projects start at €50,000. One extra client pays for the website many times over.
  • They focus on the work, not on marketing. Construction companies are busy on job sites. Most do not think about how their website brings clients, so a clear proposal stands out.
  • The value is easy to explain. Even two extra clients a month, converted through the website, is a great deal for them. Any business owner understands that.

I also chose a country: Germany, where companies can afford a professional website. The whole deal happened remotely, by email and by call.

The 4 tests for your niche

  1. High value per client. One new client is worth much more than your price.
  2. Weak websites. On Google Maps, many sites in this industry look old, unclear, or hard to use on a phone.
  3. Easy to reach. You can find an email or a contact form on their website.
  4. You understand how they win clients. You know what they need to show: past work, process, trust.

Choose a country, too

Pick a market where businesses can pay for a professional website, and where you can write emails they understand. You do not need to live there: everything happens by email and video call.

The rule

One niche, one message, one portfolio. Test it on at least 50 companies before you judge it.

02 Step two

Build a portfolio for your niche

Your portfolio has one job: make a business owner think “this person understands my business”.

It does not need to impress other designers. My first portfolios had lots of animations and visual effects. They looked cool and explained nothing. The one that worked was simple, clean, and easy to understand in a few seconds.

What goes on it

  1. A headline that names your niche and the result you bring.
  2. One or two case studies written for your niche. This is the most important part.
  3. Your process in 3 to 5 steps, so the client knows what working with you looks like.
  4. A short about section, human and simple.
  5. A contact button on every page.
Headline examples

“Websites that bring construction companies more qualified clients.”

“I design websites for architects that turn visitors into project requests.”

“Clear, modern websites for law firms that want more consultations.”

The case study, even with no client

I had no past client when I signed my first deal. My case study was a concept project: a redesign for a construction company, built to show how I think. Here is the structure I used.

PartWhat I wrote
ProblemThe company does excellent work, but its website does not show the quality of its services.
SolutionRebuild the site structure, show their projects better, explain their work process, and add a form that qualifies prospects by budget and timeline.
Pages shownThe projects page, the about page, and the key numbers.
Expected resultA clearer site can bring 1 to 2 more prospects per month, which can mean up to 2 more clients.

The expected result is what sells. A business owner does not buy a nice design. They buy more clients.

Example: a concept case study, filled in

Concept project · Musterbau GmbH, Munich (fictional company)

Problem. Musterbau builds high-end family homes. Their website shows three small photos, no project details, and only a phone number. Visitors cannot see the quality of the work, and cannot ask for a quote online.

Solution. A projects page with one detailed page per house: photos, size, duration, what the client wanted. A 4-step process page: first call, plan, build, handover. A quote form that asks for the project type, the budget range and the start date.

Pages shown. Home, Projects, Project detail, Process, Contact.

Expected result. If the new site brings 1 extra qualified request a month and Musterbau signs 1 in 4, that is 3 new projects a year. At €50,000 or more per project, the website pays for itself with the first one.

Be honest

Label it clearly as a concept: write “Concept project” on the case study, and use words like “estimated” or “could” for the result. Never invent a client, a logo or results. A good concept, well explained, is enough to get a call. That is exactly what happened to me.

03 Step three

Find prospects

I found every prospect on Google Maps. I contacted exactly 50 construction companies in Germany.

How to search

  1. Open Google Maps and search your niche plus a city. Use the local language: in Germany, search “Bauunternehmen München” rather than “construction company Munich”.
  2. Open each result and look at the website.
  3. Keep the companies whose website does not match their work.
  4. Find a contact email: the contact page, the footer, or the legal notice page (called “Impressum” in Germany). It often gives the owner’s name too.
  5. Add each company to your tracker (Template 4), with one specific problem you noticed on their site.

    04 Step four

    Approach with a cold email

    One email to each company. One follow-up two days later. My only reply came after the follow-up.

    Without the follow-up, I would still have zero clients. Many people miss the first email, or read it and forget to answer.

    The goal of the first email

    Your first email has one goal: get the person to open your portfolio. Do not try to sell the website in the email.

    The 5 rules

    1. Short. Readable on a phone in 20 seconds.
    2. About them, not about you.
    3. One specific problem you saw on their website.
    4. One link: your portfolio or your case study.
    5. One simple question at the end.

    The follow-up

    Two days after the first email, reply in the same thread with 2 or 3 lines. Remind them who you are and ask your question again.

    Your sending schedule

    WhenWhat you do
    Days 1 to 5Send 10 emails a day.
    Days 3 to 7Follow up each email 2 days after you sent it.
    Day 8Count the replies and update your tracker.

    Example email

    First email (fictional example)
    SubjectQuick idea for Musterbau’s website

    Hi Mr. Mustermann,

    I came across Musterbau on Google Maps. Your family homes in Munich look excellent, and your reviews say the same.

    But your website does not show that quality. Your projects are hard to find, and there is no simple way to ask for a quote.

    I help construction companies turn their website into a source of qualified requests. Here is a case study I made for a company like yours: [link]

    Would you be open to a 15-minute call this week?

    Best regards, Fred [portfolio link]

    Follow-up, 2 days later, in the same thread

    Hi Mr. Mustermann,

    Just following up on my message. I think Musterbau could get more qualified requests with a clearer website.

    Would a 15-minute call this week work for you?

    Best regards, Fred

    Before you hit send

    Use a professional address, ideally on your own domain. Send plain text, with no attachment and one link. Write short, simple sentences, in English or in the client’s language if you can.

    05 Step five

    Present on the discovery call

    The client asked me one question: “Have you done a website like this before?” I had not. I answered with my case study, and we signed.

    Before the call: 15 minutes

    During the call

    1. Let them talk first. Ask how they get clients today, what projects they want more of, and what they think of their website.
    2. Connect their answers to your case study. Show why they need a better site, how you would build it, and what it would bring to their business.
    3. Talk results, not design. Use their numbers: if one project is worth €50,000, one extra client already pays for the site.
    4. Agree on the next step before you hang up: you send a quote within 48 hours.

    “Have you done this before?”

    Every beginner fears this question. Do not lie, and do not panic. I showed my case study. It explained, step by step, why a new site, how the site would work, and what it would add to their business. The client did not need a past project. They needed to believe I understood their problem. Say it simply, then share your screen and walk them through your case study. The full call script, with a timer, is in Template 2.

    What you can say

    “Not yet for a paying client. But I built this case study for a construction company like yours. Let me show you how this structure would bring you more qualified requests.”

    06 Step six

    Price your project

    $7,000 for a full website: all pages, delivered in 2 months, with a quote sent before we started.

    The price made sense because of what one new client is worth to a construction company.

    Price on value, not on hours

    A beginner thinks: “I am new, so I must be cheap.” The client does not think about your experience. They compare your price to what the website brings them.

    The value math

    A simple rule of thumb

    Price the website at around 10 to 15% of what one new client is worth to your prospect. My $7,000 was roughly 13% of one €50,000 project.

    What my $7,000 included

    • every page of the site,
    • the full build,
    • the mockups, approved before the build,
    • delivery within 2 months.

    Write down what is included, and what is not: hosting fees, photos, texts, changes after delivery. An unclear scope is the first cause of problems with a client.

    How to say your price

    1. Start from the value: what is one new client worth to them?
    2. Say the number once, with confidence, then explain what it includes.
    3. Do not apologize, and do not drop your price. If the budget is too tight, reduce the scope: fewer pages now, the rest in a second phase.

    If they ask “How much?” on the call

    What you can say

    “For a full website like this, my projects are around . I’ll send you an exact quote with everything included within 48 hours.”

    Then move on: do not negotiate on the call. You do not have to start at $7,000, but price on the value you bring, not on how new you feel. Send the quote within 48 hours, using Template 3.

    07 Step seven

    Sign and get paid

    A $1,500 deposit first. Mockups until the client loved the vision. Then the build, and the remaining $5,500.

    The payment plan

    StageWhat happensPayment
    1. SignatureContract signed, project starts deposit
    2. MockupsI design the key pages until the client approves the vision—
    3. BuildI build the full site—
    4. DeliveryThe site is delivered balance

    Why this works

    • Never start the mockups before the deposit has arrived.
    • The deposit proves the client is serious and protects your time.
    • The mockups let the client see the result before you spend weeks building.
    • Once the mockups are approved, the build has fewer surprises.

    Your contract: one or two pages

    I am not a lawyer. If you are unsure, have your contract checked by a professional in your country.

    Getting paid from abroad

    Agree on the payment method before you start: bank transfer, Wise, Payoneer or PayPal are common options. Check what works between your two countries, compare the fees, and test it with the deposit, not at the end.

    Turn client 1 into client 2

    1. Ask for results. A month after launch, ask what changed: more calls, more quote requests, better prospects.
    2. Ask for a testimonial. Two or three sentences with their name and company, with their permission (Template 6).
    3. Replace your concept case study with this real one. Real numbers from a real client are your strongest sales tool.
    4. Go back to Step 3. Write to the same niche again, this time with a real result you can name.

    Your second client is easier than your first. Now you have proof.

    If it does not work yet

    Silence is not failure. It is information. Change one thing at a time.

    No replies after 50 emails

    Check three things, in this order. Is your niche high-value? Does each email name one specific problem on their website? Does your portfolio speak to that niche in 10 seconds? Fix one thing, then send the next 50.

    “Not interested.”

    That is normal. Thank them, and ask if you can check back in three months. Keep them in your tracker.

    “It’s too expensive.”

    Do not cut your price: cut the scope. Offer a first phase with the key pages, and the rest later.

    “Do you have real client projects?”

    Show your concept case study and explain your reasoning. Then offer to design the homepage mockup first, right after the deposit. That is how my client saw the vision before the build.

    They do not show up to the call

    Send a short, friendly message the same day with two new time slots.

    “My English is not good enough.”

    Mine is not perfect either. Use short sentences, check them with a spelling tool, and keep the structure of the templates. Clients care about clarity, not style.
